
Objective of the Project:

The objective of this project is to design and implement an IoT-based automation system for smart lecture halls and laboratories, aimed at optimizing energy usage by controlling lights and fans through motion detection and occupancy tracking. The system will utilize ESP32 and motion sensors to monitor human presence, count the number of people entering and exiting the room, and display this count in real-time. Additionally, the system will incorporate a delay mechanism to prevent lights and fans from turning off immediately if no motion is detected while people are still present in the room.

Objective of the Project:

The objective of this project is to design and implement an IoT-based automation system for smart lecture halls and laboratories, aimed at optimizing energy usage by controlling lights and fans through motion detection and occupancy tracking. The system will utilize ESP32 and motion sensors to monitor human presence, count the number of people entering and exiting the room, and display this count in real-time. Key objectives include:

Motion-Based Automation: Control the operation of lights and fans using motion sensors, ensuring that electrical devices are only active when the room is occupied.
Occupancy Tracking: Accurately count and display the number of people entering and exiting the room, with the count updated in real-time.
Intelligent Delay Mechanism: Implement a 10-minute delay to prevent lights and fans from turning off immediately if no motion is detected, ensuring that human presence is accounted for even in periods of inactivity.
Energy Efficiency: Automatically shut down all lights and fans if the room remains unoccupied after the 10-minute delay, reducing energy wastage.
Network Communication: Use a network transfer protocol to control the system remotely, enabling monitoring and control through IoT technology.
Scalable Solution: Provide a reliable and scalable system for automating lecture halls and labs with minimal manual intervention.

Problem Statement:

In educational institutions and workplaces, lecture halls and laboratories often experience significant energy wastage due to the manual control of lights and fans. These spaces are frequently left with electrical appliances running, even when unoccupied, leading to unnecessary power consumption. Traditional systems rely on manual intervention to turn off lights and fans, which is inefficient and prone to human error. Additionally, motion-based automation systems, while reducing energy wastage, often turn off devices prematurely when there is no detected movement, despite the presence of occupants who remain stationary for extended periods.

Thus, the challenge is to design an IoT-based system that integrates motion sensors, people-counting mechanisms, and a time-delay feature to efficiently manage the power usage of lights and fans in smart lecture halls and labs, while preventing premature shutdowns and maximizing energy savings.








Proposed System:

The proposed system aims to develop an IoT-based smart automation solution for controlling lights and fans in lecture halls and laboratories. The system will use ESP32, motion sensors, and a people-counting mechanism to monitor room occupancy and ensure efficient energy usage. The core components and functionality of the proposed system are as follows:
The proposed IoT-based system automates the control of lights and fans in smart lecture halls and labs using an ESP32 microcontroller, motion sensors, and a people-counting mechanism. The system tracks room occupancy by counting people entering and exiting, and detects motion to control devices accordingly. If no motion is detected, it waits for 10 minutes before turning off the lights and fans, preventing premature shutdowns while ensuring energy efficiency. The system supports network communication for real-time monitoring and remote control, providing a scalable, cost-effective solution for optimizing energy use in educational and corporate spaces.
Hardware Components:
ESP32 Microcontroller: The ESP32 will serve as the central processing unit, responsible for handling data from the sensors, controlling the lights and fans, and communicating over the network.
PIR Motion Sensors: These sensors will detect human movement inside the room. When motion is detected, the system will keep the lights and fans turned on.
Infrared (IR) Sensors or Ultrasonic Sensors for People Counting: These sensors will be placed at the entry and exit points to count the number of people entering and leaving the room. The count will increase as people enter and decrease as they exit, ensuring accurate real-time tracking of room occupancy.
Relay Modules: The relays will act as switches, controlling the power supply to the lights and fans based on the inputs received from the microcontroller.










Advantages of the Proposed System:

Energy Efficiency: The system reduces energy wastage by automatically turning off lights and fans when no motion is detected, ensuring that electrical devices are only used when the room is occupied.

Accurate Occupancy Detection: By using people-counting sensors at the entry and exit points, the system tracks the exact number of occupants, ensuring more precise control of lights and fans based on actual room usage.

Prevents Premature Shutdown: The 10-minute delay ensures that lights and fans remain on even when occupants are present but stationary, preventing the inconvenience of premature shutdowns during periods of inactivity.

Automation and Convenience: The system eliminates the need for manual intervention, automatically controlling room lighting and ventilation based on occupancy, enhancing user comfort and convenience.

Remote Monitoring and Control: Through IoT-based communication (e.g., MQTT), users can remotely monitor and control the system, allowing for real-time adjustments and centralized management across multiple rooms or buildings.

Cost-Effective: The use of ESP32 and affordable sensors makes the system economical, providing significant energy savings and quick return on investment, especially for large institutions like universities and offices.

Scalability: The system is modular and scalable, making it suitable for various environments such as lecture halls, labs, conference rooms, and even homes, with the ability to integrate additional features if needed.

Sustainability: By optimizing energy usage, the system contributes to sustainability goals, reducing the overall carbon footprint of institutions and helping meet environmental standards.










Limitations of the Proposed System:

Motion Sensor Limitations: The PIR motion sensors may struggle to detect occupants if they remain stationary for long periods, potentially leading to incorrect assumptions about room vacancy despite the presence of people.

Counting Accuracy: The accuracy of people counting may be affected by simultaneous entry and exit through doorways, which could lead to incorrect occupancy data, especially in high-traffic areas.

Dependence on Network Connectivity: The system’s ability to communicate and be controlled remotely relies on a stable network connection. Any network failure could disrupt the system's remote monitoring and control features











The methodology for the "IoT-Based Motion Sensor Controlled Automatic Shutdown of All Lights and Fans in Smart Lecture Halls and Labs" is centered on integrating hardware components, real-time sensor data, and network communication for automated control. The core system utilizes an ESP32 microcontroller connected to PIR motion sensors and people-counting sensors (IR or ultrasonic) to track room occupancy and detect human motion. The ESP32 processes the sensor data and controls relays that switch lights and fans on or off.

The system operates by counting individuals as they enter and exit the room using sensors placed at the entry and exit points. This data is stored and processed by the ESP32. If the room is occupied and motion is detected, the lights and fans remain on. If no motion is detected, the system activates a 10-minute delay before turning off the lights and fans, ensuring no premature shutdowns while people are still present but inactive.

Network communication is handled using the MQTT protocol, enabling real-time data transfer and remote control. Users can monitor and manage the system through a mobile app or web interface, making it highly flexible and scalable.

Programming the ESP32 includes logic for controlling devices, people counting, motion detection, and implementing the time delay. Testing and calibration are performed to ensure accuracy in sensor readings and system functionality. Once calibrated, the system is optimized for energy efficiency, with evaluations showing energy savings by reducing unnecessary use of lights and fans.

Overall, this methodology achieves an efficient, cost-effective, and scalable solution for smart automation in lecture halls and labs, combining IoT technology, real-time monitoring, and energy conservation
